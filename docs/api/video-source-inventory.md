# Video source inventory

Content imports may opt into `videoSourceProfile: "iso-bmff-v1"`. For effective slide-local pictures with a direct DrawingML `videoFile`, Parser inventories each direct reference, each `p14:media` under the specified Office extension and each `a:blip` poster reference. Each record identifies the slide, raw source element and relationship, object identity, declaration and status. It scans pictures in groups even when their geometry cannot be projected. Inactive MCE choices, masters, layouts, non-picture objects, `quickTimeFile`-only and media-extension-only objects are outside this profile.

Internal `video/mp4` targets are inspected with the bounded [ISO BMFF framing inventory](iso-bmff-inspection.md); `iso-bmff-framing` means only that top-level framing and the `ftyp` declaration were read. The record contains the part name, content type, major/minor brand, compatible brands and box count. When the shared bounded movie-clock inspection succeeds, `movieDurationMs` also records its rounded movie-header duration; otherwise the field is absent and the framing status is unchanged. This clock may differ from track or edited playback duration. The inventory does **not** verify tracks, codec, offsets, visual content or playback. Other MIME types, malformed framing, missing relationships, fragments and wrong relation types/modes are reported unsupported. Office companion records identify whether a single unambiguous internal video declaration points to the same part, a different part, or cannot be compared. Conflicting audio or QuickTime declarations block that comparison.

Poster records identify the distinct image relationship and source part or unresolved external target. `internal` describes relationship resolution only; it does not validate the poster's image bytes or imply that an image was projected. If the source has no `a:blip`, no poster record is fabricated. External references record the unresolved relationship target but are **never fetched**. Multiple objects referencing one video retain separate source records and share one inspection, charged once by part identity. Distinct inspected video parts are bounded by `maxImportedAssetBytes`, `maxTotalImportedAssetBytes` and the 64 MiB inspector limit; this inventory's aggregate counter is separate from image projection and audio inventory. Each profile enforces its own `maxReportFindings` cap. Limits fail the import rather than truncating the report.

The inventory alone creates no Core video asset, grants no source editing or playback permission, does not extract a poster, and does not alter the default static media-poster fallback. No-op save retains the original source bytes. Default imports omit `videoSources`. The Node file adapter captures the option before I/O; Node and Chromium inventories are compared by a no-network regression test. See the [native H.264 observation](../testing/native-h264-video.md) for the specific source sample, not a general compatibility claim.

## Explicit object projection and authoring

`videoProjection: "iso-bmff-v1"` also enables this inventory and projects a picture with one unambiguous internal `videoFile`, nonzero mapped geometry and either no Office companion or a single same-part internal companion. A PowerPoint-trimmed video with the exact external `NULL` direct target can instead use one embedded MP4 companion when its nonzero integral trim is valid and its movie duration is known; this sentinel is never fetched. The video bytes must have the declared `video/mp4` type and pass the top-level framing inspection above; this is **not codec or playback validation**. A representable stretched, opaque poster becomes a separate image asset; an unavailable poster produces a video object without a poster. Unsupported mapped poster geometry blocks video projection. Identical source part uses share a single video asset, while identities of different source parts remain distinct. Projected video, audio and image parts share the imported-asset aggregate budget, in addition to the independent source-inventory scan budgets.

Core represents videos as `type: "video"` with a separately registered `kind: "video"` asset and optional image poster. Byte registration snapshots the input; reference checks, cleanup and cross-document copy include both IDs, including hidden and nested objects. Geometry and rotation flow through Layout. SVG shows the poster stretched to the object frame, or a placeholder when absent, and emits `video-static-preview`; it neither resolves the video resource nor plays it. The poster itself follows the ordinary image resolver policy. The projection cannot be combined with edit-save or picture-source profiles. A no-op save still retains original bytes. Node and Chromium projection and static preview have no-network parity coverage.

PPTX generation rejects video by default. The experimental `videoMode: "iso-bmff-v1"` accepts `video/mp4` bytes embedded in the asset or supplied by the explicit [media resolver](byte-assets.md#external-audio-and-video), with bounded top-level framing, exactly one `moov` and nonempty `mdat`, no `moof` fragment, and one version-zero, zero-flags `mvhd` with a known positive movie duration. The writer uses the shared [movie-clock inspection](iso-bmff-inspection.md#bounded-movie-clock) and its time scale and duration to create native timing; the movie duration may differ from any particular track or edit list. It checks child framing and limits the movie to 4096 direct children, but does not validate tracks, codecs, offsets or playback. This reading follows the [movie-header field layout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quicktime-file-format/movie_header_atom); unsupported headers fail rather than guessing a duration.

Up to 4096 visible, opaque top-level videos per slide with nonzero geometry are accepted, without actions, placeholder bindings or authored event timelines. Same-slide PCM audio is supported when both `videoMode: "iso-bmff-v1"` and `audioMode: "wave-pcm-v1"` are explicitly enabled; each profile's own constraints and limits still apply. The main play sequence follows element order, with independent interactive pause targets for videos. Grouped, hidden and definition-owned videos are rejected before asset resolution. The same video asset may be shared on or across slides. Supplied posters follow image loading policy and must resolve; without one the writer supplies a neutral placeholder, never extracts a frame. Output carries `video-playback-unverified`, and generates paired video/media relationships, a poster and separate click/interactive targets based on the observed native samples. Unreferenced video assets are not loaded. Bounded click/automatic main-sequence starts are projected when the complete source sequence matches projected media order; other source playback settings and source edit-save are not mapped; general codecs and broader native compatibility remain open.


Video objects can set `playbackStart` to `"click-sequence"` (the default), `"automatic"` or `"object-click"`. An automatic first effect begins with the main sequence; a later automatic effect follows the preceding main effect after its written duration, including an audio effect. Independent audio and video object clicks are excluded. Object-click video uses a separately targeted `togglePause` interaction with no main play command, matching one observed native form; repeated-click and actual playback behavior remain unverified. Automatic video cannot follow an indefinitely looping main audio effect. The native command duration is the movie-header duration, which can differ from actual edited or track playback. The matching Parser projection requires the entire supported sequence, supported video trim metadata, unique identities, verified video pause chains, known audio settings and matching durations in drawing order. Object-click projection requires a complete unambiguous direct media node and matching independent click chain, with no conflicting command. Other video starts and source-preserving edits are not supported. In PowerPoint for Mac 16.104 a generated two-video file opened without a visible repair prompt and both objects showed “Automatically”; actual playback, re-entry and other applications remain unverified. See [the named native observation](../testing/native-media-start.md#generated-video-automatic-start).


Videos may set `trimStartMs` and `trimEndMs` as integer milliseconds removed from the beginning and end of the source. Each defaults to zero and explicit zeros retain default export bytes. The sum must leave at least one millisecond of the rounded movie-header duration, checked per object after byte inspection even for shared or resolved resources. The full MP4 remains embedded; the written command duration and subsequent automatic delay use the retained interval. Import projects a single valid integral trim when the movie clock is known; fractional, ambiguous, unknown-duration or out-of-range values are not guessed. The movie clock may differ from track, edit-list or actual playback duration. A generated interval was recognized in a named PowerPoint trim dialog, but actual playback, other applications and source edit-save remain unverified. See [the native observation](../testing/native-video-trim.md).
